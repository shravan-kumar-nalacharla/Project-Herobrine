- Overworld is uninhabitable at the story's narrated starting frame.
- Paradise exists and is considered humanity's last refuge.
- Paradise has a stated 43-year relationship to the Overworld.
- Nether species unite in an apparent campaign against Paradise.
- Steve, Alex, and the creator ensemble belong to the conflict.
- Wither/Wither Skeleton/Piglin-scale war imagery occurs.
- Paradise undergoes catastrophic environmental conversion.
- Alex is shown fallen/injured; her death is not confirmed.
- RawKnee vandalized a Herobrine statue and names Chapati as innocent.
- Bixu questions RawKnee; RawKnee denies that the Nether is the true cause.
- The question of who is behind everything remains open.

### Soft canon

- Paradise is literally a separate universe.
- The Nether is morally responsible for the war.
- Steve's public heroic reputation is objectively accurate.
- The sacred light is benevolent.
- Montage events occur in the order shown.
- Every ensemble skin is present in the same scene.
- The “hundredfold” force is Herobrine.
- Alex is dead.

### Fair misdirection opportunities

- Paradise's “reflection” is a reconstruction, rollback, or maintained copy rather than time travel.
- Nether “invaders” are displaced originals or refugees.
- The Herobrine statue is a technical node misidentified as an idol.
- RawKnee's vandalism does not summon Herobrine; it disables observation and permits another culprit to act.
- The sacred light that preserves Paradise may be what destroys Nether habitats.
- Steve's legend conceals a past failure.
- Herobrine's hundredfold response may save Paradise while appearing to attack it.
- June 31 is an extra day outside recorded causality.
