MINECRAFT
THE KNOT
MASTER STORY TREATMENT & SERIES BIBLE
“Every choice becomes someone else’s fate.”
A nonlinear Minecraft mystery about a world that becomes its own future, a hero who becomes his own antagonist, and a five-minute decision that changes civilization.

The one image that contains the whole story
Alex sees a baby villager running for its life while the Sun is falling toward the Overworld. Steve tells her to keep running toward the portal. She turns back for the child. That decision appears to destroy her life. By the finale, we discover it helped create the civilization Steve was born into.
1. THE PHILOSOPHICAL ENGINE
Cause and effect
The universe of The Knot is governed by causality. Every action becomes the condition another person must live inside. A choice is never isolated; it travels outward through people, generations and time.
Every decision for something is a decision against something else.
As you sow, so shall you reap.
Our destiny is nothing but a connection of cause and effect.
Fate versus choice
The story should initially present fate and freedom as opposites. Steve believes either history is fixed or it can be changed. His final realization is more difficult: fate is the structure produced when countless choices collide. Certain events become so entangled with everything after them that removing the event removes the people who came from it.
Desire as compass
Steve is free to pursue Alex, but he did not choose to love her, to fear losing her, or to feel guilty. The story repeatedly asks where choice actually begins if the desire behind a decision was itself caused by things we never chose.
Man can do what he wills, but he cannot will what he wills.
Perspective
Ten witnesses can describe the same elephant from ten angles and all appear to contradict one another while each holds part of the truth. The series uses this principle structurally: the Sunfall is shown repeatedly through different eyes, and the audience only understands the event once those perspectives are assembled.
Knowledge comes at a cost
Every major answer creates a worse question. Steve learns Alex survived - but she is trapped millennia in the past. He learns the catastrophe can be prevented - but preventing it could erase entire civilizations. He learns the Black Traveller is not his enemy - but his future self.
The truth is a strange thing. You can suppress it, bury it and replace it with a lie, but eventually it finds its way back to the surface.
2. THE SECRET COSMOLOGY
The three worlds
The greatest world-building reveal is that the three Minecraft dimensions are connected states in one cosmic history rather than unrelated places.
Portals
Portals do not merely connect coordinates. They connect states of the universe. The ancient Builders discovered that Ender matter can bend the field in which space, time and mass exist. A portal is therefore a wound in causality: step through the wrong portal at the wrong moment and you may arrive not somewhere else, but somewhen else.
The Sunfall
The catastrophe is caused by a massive celestial near-pass. Alex and the ancient Builders construct the Anchor to push the Sun farther away. The intervention instead pulls it toward the Overworld. The warning causes the disaster it was meant to prevent.
The Anchor
The Anchor is an ancient redstone-Ender machine capable of applying tiny gravitational changes to enormous celestial bodies. Its original design is a bootstrap paradox: Steve learns the plans from the future, sends them backward, Alex helps build them in the past, and those ancient plans later become the future source Steve learns from. No inventor exists.
Causal knots
Some moments cannot be removed without erasing everything that grew from them. These are not magically predetermined events; they are points with so many downstream consequences that the universe strongly “pulls” history back toward an equivalent outcome.
3. PROLOGUE - THE LAST FIVE MINUTES
Open on total normality. Villagers trade. A farmer plants wheat. A child chases an animal. Steve and Alex are walking home. Then every animal stops moving and looks toward the sky.
The shadows change direction.
Steve looks up. The Sun is impossibly large.
The sky turns red. Water rises from lakes in trembling columns. Blocks split. Mountains groan. The world begins to pull upward.
Steve and Alex sprint toward a ruined portal they discovered earlier. Then Alex sees a baby villager running in the wrong direction, alone.
STEVE: “ALEX! DON’T STOP!”
Alex stops anyway. She turns back, reaches the child, lifts him and runs toward the portal. Steve activates it. The gravitational wind becomes violent enough to tear blocks from the ground.
Steve stretches his hand toward Alex. Their fingers almost touch. Steve is thrown backward through the portal. Alex and the child are lifted into the air.
The Sun descends.
Then everything freezes: fire, rain, debris, people - even Steve’s falling body. Only one figure can move.
A human-shaped entity made of black matter stands in the sky. Black particles stream from its body. It raises one hand toward the Sun.
The Sun stops.
Steve cannot see the entity’s face.
This exact five-minute sequence should return in every episode, each time from a different point of view. The audience gradually discovers that almost every assumption formed during the opening is wrong or incomplete.
4. SEASON ONE - EPISODE BREAKDOWN
EPISODE 1 - FIVE MINUTES
Premise: Steve wakes after falling through the portal. He assumes he is in the Nether, another dimension. He is alone and believes Alex is dead.
Discovery: He finds a ruined village beneath lava. A mountain has the same silhouette as the mountain beside his Overworld home. Beneath the Nether terrain he digs up stone bricks and the bell from his village.
Reveal: The Nether is not another world. It is his world, thousands of years later.
Closing beat: A voice in the darkness says, “You want to know what happened here? You did.” Black particles vanish before Steve can turn.
EPISODE 2 - HELL IS EMPTY
Premise: Steve encounters Piglins, initially presented as monsters. He discovers they preserve oral histories of blue skies, oceans and the Sunfall.
Myth: Piglin legends describe a figure called the Black Traveller, a being who appeared before the heavens burned. They believe he promised salvation and brought destruction.
Character shift: Steve stops seeing Piglins as enemies and begins seeing them as descendants of survivors. The Nether is someone’s homeland, not merely Steve’s hell.
Symbol: Steve recovers a compass that always points toward the place where Alex disappeared. He treats it as a physical guide, but it represents desire controlling his life.
Line: “You call this Hell because you were lucky enough to know Heaven.”
EPISODE 3 - TEN WITNESSES
Structure: The Sunfall is reconstructed through conflicting accounts: villager religion, Piglin history, Builder science, Steve’s memory and Enderman fragments.
Theme: Everyone describes the same elephant from a different angle. Nobody is lying. Nobody possesses the whole truth.
Discovery: Steve reaches a colossal temple carved downward from a mountain. On a wall thousands of years old is an image of Alex holding a child.
Reveal: Alex did not die. She travelled backward in time.
EPISODE 4 - THE CARRIER
Perspective: The story finally follows Alex after the opening catastrophe.
Journey: The temporal rupture throws Alex and the baby villager thousands of years into the past. There are no modern villages or established civilization.
Aren: Alex raises the child, eventually named Aren. He grows into the First Cartographer, connecting settlements through roads, maps, agriculture and trade.
Paradox: The civilization that eventually produces baby Aren exists because adult Aren helped create it. Aren becomes a bootstrap paradox in human form.
Legacy: Alex becomes remembered as The Carrier - the traveller who appeared from nowhere carrying a child.
EPISODE 5 - KNOWLEDGE IS A CURSE
Discovery: Steve finds the ancient Builder chamber containing gravitational calculations, redstone systems and Ender-field equations.
The Anchor: The Builders constructed a machine capable of changing a celestial body’s trajectory. Alex helped them because she knew the Sunfall was coming.
Paradox: Steve recognizes the plans because he already encountered the same designs in the future and sent them into the past. Nobody invented the Anchor.
Dread: Steve realizes Alex’s attempt to prevent the catastrophe may have created it.
EPISODE 6 - THE BLACK TRAVELLER
Conflict: Steve hunts and finally fights the entity he believes caused everything. Their battle jumps through unstable portals: ancient Overworld, present Overworld, Nether and fragments of the End.
Reveal: Steve tears away part of the entity’s mask. The Black Traveller is an older Steve, scarred and partially transformed by Ender matter.
Dialogue: Future Steve: “I have already stood where you are standing.” Present Steve: “Then tell me how to stop this.” Future Steve: “That’s what I said.”
Truth: Every attempt Future Steve made to escape the catastrophe became another cause leading toward it.
EPISODE 7 - THE LABYRINTH
Plan: Present Steve decides to destroy the Anchor before it activates.
Moral problem: Without the Sunfall, Alex never travels into the past, Aren never becomes the First Cartographer, Piglin history never develops as known, and countless descendants may never exist.
Conflict: Future Steve protects the loop. Present Steve calls him a monster for allowing suffering. Future Steve accuses Present Steve of being willing to erase billions of people because they are not the people he remembers.
Line: “You want to save your world. I want to save the people your world forgot.”
Realization: Steve has treated history as two choices: preserve catastrophe or prevent catastrophe. The labyrinth has another possibility - the centre.
EPISODE 8 - THE CENTRE
Structure: Past Alex, Present Steve and Future Steve act simultaneously as the Anchor folds three periods together.
Solution: Steve does not prevent the Sunfall. He changes its severity. Certain causal events remain, but their exact form can change.
Sunfall truth: Future Steve was never holding the Sun still. He was changing its trajectory by a fraction of a degree. Across astronomical distance, that fraction saves the planet from complete annihilation.
Transformation: The world still burns and eventually becomes the Nether, preserving the causal chain, but life survives.
Emotional reveal: Steve discovers Alex’s final journal. She lived sixty-three years in the ancient world. She asks him not to erase an entire lifetime because he cannot forgive himself for missing five minutes of it.
Final image: Years later the compass that always pointed toward the moment Steve lost Alex finally turns away from the past and points forward.
5. THE CAUSAL REVEALS
Reveal 1 - The Nether is the future
The audience first believes Steve escaped into another dimension. Geographic repetition, buried ruins and the village bell reveal that he travelled into the future. This transforms the Nether from a fantasy location into evidence of a tragedy.
Reveal 2 - Alex lived
Alex’s disappearance is not death. She falls into the ancient past, where she lives an entire life. Steve’s grief is based on a true loss but an incomplete story.
Reveal 3 - The helpless baby matters more than Steve realizes
The child Alex turns back to save becomes Aren, the First Cartographer, whose work helps form the village network. What appears to be a five-second mistake becomes a civilization-level act.
Reveal 4 - The villain is Steve
The Black Traveller is Future Steve. Scenes that previously seemed like attacks can be reinterpreted as protection, manipulation or attempts to preserve the conditions needed for Future Steve to exist.
Reveal 5 - The warning caused the disaster
The Sun was going to pass dangerously close. The Anchor was built because future knowledge warned Alex of catastrophe. The Anchor’s intervention pulls the Sun closer and creates the catastrophe it was designed to prevent.
Reveal 6 - There is no first inventor
The Anchor design is passed from future to past and later rediscovered as ancient knowledge. It exists inside a closed causal loop. The audience is forced to confront a thing with information but no origin.
Reveal 7 - Fate can change without disappearing
Steve finally escapes the binary. He cannot erase the Sunfall without erasing lives caused by it, but he can change the catastrophe’s magnitude. The same causal knot can be tied differently.
Reveal 8 - Steve misunderstood Alex’s tragedy
“You remember the five minutes in which you lost me. I remember the sixty-three years I lived afterward.”
The emotional climax is not saving Alex. It is Steve accepting that Alex’s life belonged to Alex. His grief does not give him the right to erase the person she became.
6. CHARACTER BIBLE
STEVE - The man who cannot stop looking backward
Beginning: brave, practical and optimistic. He believes every problem has a solution if he works hard enough.
Middle: grief becomes guilt, guilt becomes obsession, and knowledge gives that obsession tools. He starts treating time itself as something that owes him a correction.
Future form: the Black Traveller has learned too much. He manipulates events because he believes preserving causality is the only way to save the people history created. To Present Steve, this looks monstrous.
Resolution: Steve understands that accepting limits is not surrender. He cannot control every consequence, but he can still choose what kind of person responds to those consequences.
ALEX - The person Steve keeps reducing to a loss
Alex must never exist only as “the woman Steve needs to save.” Her independent life in the ancient world is essential. She becomes an engineer, explorer, teacher, mother figure, historian and one of the architects of the civilization Steve inherits.
Her greatest narrative purpose is to challenge Steve’s assumption that separation equals death. The viewer eventually realizes that while Steve spent decades mourning five minutes, Alex spent decades living.
AREN - The child who becomes an ancestor of his own world
Aren begins as a terrified baby villager. Raised by Alex, he grows fascinated by maps because he has no memory of where he came from. He becomes the First Cartographer, linking isolated communities and teaching them that survival depends on connection.
AREN: “Nothing survives alone.”
Aren is the most human expression of the bootstrap paradox: he is born from a civilization that he later helps create.
FUTURE STEVE / THE BLACK TRAVELLER
For most of the season he should feel terrifying: appearing where he should not exist, attacking Steve, erasing evidence, saving enemies and apparently causing disasters.
After the reveal, every action must remain morally ambiguous. He is not secretly good in a simple sense. He has become willing to manipulate his younger self and permit terrible suffering because he has seen the lives that vanish in alternate histories.
THE ENDERMAN
Use one recurring Enderman as a mysterious observer. It knows fragments of the causal structure but refuses to explain events in human categories such as beginning/end, good/evil, before/after. Its dialogue should be sparse and unsettling.
ENDERMAN: “You are still searching for beginnings.”
7. SYMBOLS AND VISUAL MOTIFS
The Triquetra
Use the three-loop symbol throughout architecture, books, portal frames and ancient carvings. Its meaning evolves: Overworld / Nether / End; Past / Present / Future; Alex / Steve / Aren; finally Cause / Effect / Observer.
The compass
Steve’s compass points toward the location/time of Alex’s disappearance. The literal object externalizes his desire. In the final scene, when Steve finally accepts the past, its needle turns forward for the first time.
The Sun
The Sun should sit behind characters during major decisions. It begins as ordinary scenery, becomes an approaching godlike threat, then becomes a physical symbol of consequences too large for one person to control.
Circles
Portal frames
The Sun
Compass faces
Eyes
Loops in the Triquetra
Circular redstone mechanisms
Water
Water represents ordinary time because it always flows. Near temporal fractures, droplets rise, rivers briefly reverse, or suspended water becomes the first visual warning that causality is being disturbed.
Black particles
Whenever Future Steve has interfered in history, a few black particles remain. Early episodes can hide them in backgrounds so rewatches reveal that the Black Traveller was present long before viewers recognized him.
The labyrinth
The ancient temple contains a real underground labyrinth. Hundreds of routes appear to offer different endings, but all eventually lead to a central chamber. The metaphor is incomplete, however: the route changes the person who reaches the centre. Destination and journey both matter.
8. NONLINEAR STORYTELLING DESIGN
Revisit the same five minutes
Each episode should contain a new version of the opening catastrophe. The scene is never repeated merely for style; each return must reveal information that changes the meaning of earlier versions.
Plant clues before explanations
Black particles appear in scenes where Future Steve is not yet visible.
The Nether mountain silhouette matches the Overworld in Episode 1 before anyone comments on it.
Ancient temple art includes the same compass Steve later carries.
An old inscription uses a phrase Alex says casually in the opening.
The Black Traveller occasionally protects a character Steve considers an enemy.
Future Steve’s injuries should match wounds Present Steve receives later, allowing attentive viewers to predict the identity reveal.
The first episode can contain a barely audible older version of Steve’s voice hidden beneath the portal sound.
Rule for twists

10. QUESTIONS THE SERIES SHOULD MAKE VIEWERS ARGUE ABOUT
If a prophecy causes people to create the event it predicts, was it ever a prophecy?
If saving someone today causes suffering centuries later, was the rescue still right?
If removing one tragedy erases millions of people born afterward, is preventing the tragedy moral?
If your future self influences your younger choices, which version of you has free will?
If desires are caused by things we never chose, how free are the decisions produced by those desires?
Does knowing your fate destroy freedom, or create the first real opportunity to resist it?
If two enemies are each protecting people they love, which one is the villain?
Can information exist without ever being invented?
Can a life be tragic from one person’s perspective and meaningful from the person who lived it?
If everything is connected, where does responsibility begin - and where does it end?
11. THE FINALE IN DETAIL
Three periods collapse into one
Inside the Anchor, past, present and future overlap. Alex in the ancient age can see fragments of Present Steve. Present Steve can hear Future Steve. Future Steve experiences memories before they happen. The editing should make the three versions feel like one argument occurring across centuries.
Steve discovers the centre
The binary problem is: activate the Anchor and preserve the catastrophe, or destroy the Anchor and erase everything caused by it. Steve realizes those are not the only options. He changes the calibration rather than the existence of the machine.
The Anchor still interacts with the Sun, preserving the chain of events that sends Alex backward and Steve forward, but Future Steve changes the final trajectory enough to prevent total planetary annihilation.
Return to the opening
The entire first scene plays again. This time, when the Black Traveller appears to hold the Sun, viewers understand the reality: he is applying a tiny correction. What looked like supernatural strength was precision.
Young Steve sees the Black Traveller through the portal. Future Steve sees his younger self. Their eyes meet. Young Steve experiences terror; Future Steve experiences recognition. It is the same moment but two entirely different emotions.
Alex’s journal
After the catastrophe, Steve finds a hidden room beneath the ancient temple. Alex lived there in old age. She knew Steve would eventually arrive and left a book for him.
“You will want to come back for me. For a long time, I wanted you to. Then I understood something. You remember the five minutes in which you lost me. I remember the sixty-three years I lived afterward.”
“Don’t erase my life because you cannot forgive yourself for missing five minutes of it.”
This is the emotional answer to the season. Steve has been treating Alex’s life as a problem in his own story. Her message forces him to recognize that her life continued beyond his observation.
The compass turns
Years later, Steve walks through a recovering Overworld. A child asks whether the future can be changed. Steve answers, “Tomorrow will already be different from today.” The child complains that this is not an answer. Steve smiles: “Exactly.”
Steve takes out Alex’s compass. For the entire season the needle pointed backward toward the location of his loss. Now it turns. It points forward.
12. POST-CREDIT SCENE & SEASON TWO SEED
Total darkness. An Enderman opens its eyes. It stands among floating islands in the void. This is The End.
An extremely old Steve walks across ruined structures containing architectural fragments from both the Overworld and Nether. He discovers the Triquetra carved into black stone, but now there is a fourth incomplete mark beside it.
A voice behind him says:
“You thought the Nether was the future.”
Steve turns.
“It was only tomorrow.”

14. WRITING & PRODUCTION RULES
Protect the emotional story
The time mechanics only matter if the audience cares about Steve, Alex and Aren. Every lore reveal should alter a relationship, a guilt, a sacrifice or a moral decision. Avoid explaining cosmology simply because it is clever.
Do not over-explain
Let visuals carry clues: geography matching across eras, recurring architecture, black particles, identical scars, ancient words that echo modern dialogue. The audience should often understand a reveal half a scene before a character says it.
Make Minecraft mechanics feel inevitable
Nether portals become temporal infrastructure rather than arbitrary fantasy doors.
Redstone becomes engineering language for the Anchor and ancient Builder machines.
Ender Pearls and Endermen become evidence of beings partially detached from ordinary space-time.
Ruined portals become archaeological scars from previous attempts to cross periods.
Compasses become both practical items and philosophical symbols of desire.
The End becomes the ultimate destination for the story’s question about endings and beginnings.
Keep the Black Traveller frightening after the reveal
Do not turn Future Steve into a simple mentor. He has manipulated deaths, hidden information and deliberately shaped his younger self. The audience should understand him without being forced to approve of him.
Use silence
Some of the strongest moments should contain almost no dialogue: Steve recognizing the village bell beneath Nether rock; seeing Alex carved into an ancient temple; discovering his own face beneath the Black Traveller mask; the compass finally turning.
Season rhythm
Episodes 1-2 establish survival mystery. Episodes 3-4 expand perspective and emotionally restore Alex. Episodes 5-6 reveal the paradox and antagonist identity. Episodes 7-8 turn the mystery into a moral choice and emotional resolution.
15. THE STORY IN ONE PAGE
Steve and Alex live in a peaceful Overworld until the Sun suddenly falls toward the planet. While fleeing through a portal, Alex turns back to save a baby villager. Steve is thrown through the portal alone and wakes in the Nether, where he slowly discovers he has not travelled to another dimension but into the burned future of his own world.
Steve becomes obsessed with returning to Alex. Ancient ruins reveal that she survived by falling thousands of years into the past with the child. Alex raises him as Aren, who becomes the First Cartographer and helps create the network of villages that eventually produces the civilization from which he himself was born.
Alex also helps ancient Builders construct the Anchor, a machine intended to prevent the future Sunfall she remembers. Steve discovers the machine’s designs are trapped in a bootstrap loop: the future gives them to the past, and the past becomes the future’s source. Worse, the Anchor’s attempt to push the Sun away is what pulls it toward the Overworld.
Steve hunts the mysterious Black Traveller whom history blames for the catastrophe and discovers the entity is his own future self. Future Steve has spent years preserving the causal loop because alternate histories that prevent the Sunfall erase entire populations born from its consequences. Present Steve wants to destroy the Anchor anyway.
At the centre of the ancient labyrinth, Steve realizes history is not limited to two choices. He cannot erase the Sunfall without erasing lives, but he can change how destructive it becomes. Past Alex, Present Steve and Future Steve work across the same folded moment. The Sun still comes. Alex still travels backward. Steve still travels forward. The Overworld still becomes the Nether. But Future Steve changes the Sun’s trajectory just enough for life to survive.
Finally, Steve finds the journal Alex left in old age. She tells him that he remembers the five minutes in which he lost her, while she remembers the sixty-three years she lived afterward. Steve understands that his grief made him treat Alex’s life as though it ended when she left his view.
Years later, the compass that always pointed toward his loss finally points forward. Steve follows it.
Every choice becomes someone else’s fate.
