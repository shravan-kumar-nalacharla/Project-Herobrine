## 2. Authority hierarchy

When sources disagree, use this order:

1. Trailer hard canon: clearly visible or audible facts.
2. Trailer soft canon: montage implications that may be recontextualized.
3. The user's direct instructions, including the full legacy SMP ensemble and the requirement that Alex, Steve, and the changing world remain central.
4. The strongest underlying ideas in `my writings.txt`.
5. The old bible, only when it survives critical review.

This prevents the old bible's self-contained time-travel plot from swallowing the much larger story promised by the trailer.

## 3. What the trailer promises

The public-facing trailer promises all of the following:

- A ruined or uninhabitable Overworld.
- A refuge called Paradise.
- Paradise is connected to the Overworld of 43 years earlier; the narration calls it a reflection, leaving the exact mechanism open.
- Paradise contains lush forests, oceans, villages, mountains, snow, strange luminous depths, and a light associated with life or sanctity.
- Humanity's last refuge is under threat from an advancing darkness.
- Multiple Nether species join in a declared war to destroy Paradise.
- A group of young people arrive in Paradise to survive.
- Steve and Alex are fundamental mythic figures, not merely cameo avatars.
- A recognizable Herobrine SMP ensemble lives, travels, sleeps, jokes, and fights in this world.
- Paradise undergoes catastrophic conversion: forests burn and oceans become lava-like fields.
- The war is explicitly framed as a struggle for the right to live, not merely territory.
- There are large armies, Piglins, Wither Skeletons, a Wither, energy or portal phenomena, mass casualties, a fallen Alex, and apocalyptic celestial imagery.
- RawKnee and I.M. Bixu share the only extended character-dialogue scene. RawKnee has desecrated a Herobrine statue and insists the destruction is not simply caused by the Nether.
- The deeper architect of the catastrophe is deliberately withheld.
- The trailer ends on symbolic images of two opposed colors/forces, a squid-like silhouette, a glitching humanoid, a suspended body, a fortress/tower, and a red celestial disc.
- The release card reads `JUNE 31, 2026`, an impossible calendar date. Whether intentional or accidental, it is now a valuable soft-canon anomaly.

The old bible does not currently pay off most of these promises.

## 7. Strongest source connections the old bible missed

1. The impossible June 31 card can become the cleanest “invisible until rewatch” clue in the entire project.
2. Paradise's 43-year offset can connect the trailer's refuge premise to the notes' causality obsession without requiring unlimited time travel.
3. RawKnee's comic confession can be fair misdirection: he did damage a statue, but the statue may be an instrument, memorial, lock, witness, or identity anchor rather than a religious idol.
4. The trailer's Nether coalition can embody the note “ten witnesses see one object differently.” Paradise calls them invaders; they may call themselves returnees, survivors, or the dispossessed.
5. The light that blesses Paradise and the darkness approaching it should be parts of one mechanism. This turns simple good-versus-evil imagery into a moral duality.
6. Alex saving a villager child can connect the private emotional story to the public war. The child should matter because of choices and relationships, not prophecy.
7. The ocean imagery and later lava conversion can be a physical record: water stores world-memory; heating or Nether conversion releases or destroys it.
8. Steve's heroic legend may be propaganda. The narrator says he made his first world an example for everyone, but the story can ask who authored that history and what it omits.

## 8. Biggest worldbuilding opportunities

- Paradise as a deliberately maintained “historical reflection,” including institutions that decide what parts of history are preserved.
- Nether peoples with internal factions, families, historians, and legitimate claims.
- A 43-year refugee generation: parents remember the Overworld; children only know Paradise or Nether.
- Minecraft mechanics as civic infrastructure: chunk borders as property and jurisdiction, respawn anchors as citizenship, inventories as legal identity, portals as controlled borders, seed archives as history.
- The sacred light as a measurable stability field tied to loaded chunks, memory, or seed fidelity.
- Villager testimony and trade records as a distributed historical archive.
- Oceans, ancient monuments, and drowned structures as repositories of pre-Paradise truth.
- Herobrine statues as a network of observation/repair nodes that different cultures interpret religiously, scientifically, and politically.

## 9. Biggest story risks

1. Ensemble overload: nine creator identities plus Alex, Steve, and Herobrine can flatten everyone. The answer is functional grouping and staggered introductions.
2. Lore replacing emotion: causality jargon cannot substitute for Alex and Steve's relationship or the ensemble's fear of losing a home.
3. Generic “simulation” reveal: avoid treating save files and chunks as a shallow Matrix metaphor.
4. Generic time-loop reveal: future Steve as the villain is coherent but familiar and too dominant in the old bible.
5. Monster stereotyping: if all Nether beings are evil, the trailer's “right to live” theme becomes hollow.
6. Power inflation: Herobrine, Wither, portals, and celestial catastrophe need costs and limitations.
7. Comedy whiplash: the RawKnee/Bixu scene is valuable human texture, but it must release tension without trivializing mass death.
8. False precision: “43 years” and “June 31” must receive deliberate payoffs; leaving them unexplained would feel careless.
9. Trademark/identity imitation: the series should use public creator personas respectfully without fabricating real-world allegations or private traits. Characterization must be fictional and in-world.
10. An impossible production promise: “best in history” is a motivating target, not a quality guarantee. The practical standard is coherent, emotional, rewatchable, and producible.

## 10. Five strongest central-mystery directions (not yet selected)

### Direction A - The Forty-Third Copy

Paradise is not the past. It is a living reconstruction generated from the Overworld's last intact seed-state, exactly 43 years out of date. Every correction required to keep it “faithful” deletes new choices. Herobrine is the presence that remembers the deleted differences.

Strengths: trailer-compatible, Minecraft-specific, emotionally potent, avoids ordinary time travel.

### Direction B - The Returning Nether

The Nether coalition is not conquering Paradise; it is trying to reclaim the only stable state of the world from which its ancestors were expelled. Both sides are descendants of the same catastrophe. Paradise's survival field worsens Nether collapse.

Strengths: turns the war into a moral conflict over survival and history.

### Direction C - The Thirty-First Day

Once every cycle, the world creates an unrecorded day between chunks of history. Events on that day have effects but no accepted cause. June 31 is not a typo inside the story; it is the only date on which someone can alter the Knot without being erased from ordinary memory.

Strengths: retroactively rewards the trailer, creates precise limitations, supports mystery structure.

### Direction D - The Witness Network

Herobrine is neither one man nor a god. The white-eyed figure is the visible form of a world-level witness assembled from discarded player memories, death states, and unclaimed inventories. Statues are dormant witness nodes. Damaging one does not summon evil; it blinds part of the world and lets someone rewrite local history.

Strengths: explains the statue, eyes, Steve-like form, limited appearances, and perspective theme.

### Direction E - The World That Chooses Back

Minecraft generation is not passive. When players load, exploit, abandon, or overwrite chunks, the world models which version of itself is most likely to survive. Paradise, Nether, and the ruined Overworld are competing survival solutions produced by one world. Herobrine is its imperfect attempt to communicate through the default human shape.

Strengths: makes the changing world a true protagonist and antagonist while remaining Minecraft-native.

## 11. Phase 1 conclusion

The strongest eventual architecture will probably hybridize A, B, C, and D: Paradise as a 43-year reconstruction; Nether peoples as displaced claimants; June 31 as the legal/causal gap in the reconstruction; and Herobrine as the witness that remembers overwritten worlds. Direction E can supply the larger philosophical layer.

This is a recommendation for Phase 2 testing, not a locked answer.
